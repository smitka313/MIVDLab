Set up your two micro:bits

One program, two BBC micro:bit V2 boards. You need one battery pack for the car board and a USB cable for the computer board. Both boards use the same HEX file.

Step 1 — Download: Download the micro:bit program (.hex). Save it to your computer.

2. Install on both boards

  1. Connect a micro:bit V2 to your computer by USB.
  2. Drag the downloaded .hex file onto its MICROBIT drive. Wait until the transfer finishes.
  3. Repeat with the second micro:bit. You do not need two different programs.

3. Set up the wireless receiver

  1. Leave one micro:bit connected to your computer via USB.
  2. Hold button A while pressing the Reset button on the back, then release A after startup. The display should show RX.
  3. Open Microsoft MakeCode's serial data viewer for live wireless values. The receiver is not the device that records the onboard CSV.

4. Set up the car recorder

  1. Connect the other micro:bit to the battery pack. Start it normally without holding A; it should show CAR.
  2. Secure the board flat to the chassis, LEDs facing up and top edge pointing forward. Keep it away from wheels and moving parts.
  3. With the car stationary on level ground, press A. Wait for CAL to finish before driving.
  4. Drive your test. Stop the vehicle and press B to end recording. The displayed mph value is only an experimental estimate.

5. Get your data into this dashboard

  1. Connect the car micro:bit to the computer by USB.
  2. Open MY_DATA on the MICROBIT drive and export its data as CSV.
  3. In this site, choose Import CSV files. The 10-column universal-program format is supported automatically.
  4. Review graphs, enter the vehicle setup, and compare trials.

What is recorded?

Each sample contains: run, time_ms, ax_mg, ay_mg, az_mg, forward_ms2, lateral_ms2, pitch_deg, roll_deg, speed_est_mph. The dashboard calculates total acceleration magnitude and a motion/vibration proxy from the raw axes, rather than expecting those missing columns.

Accuracy note: This setup does not measure true speed, distance, battery energy, or reliable tilt during fast maneuvers. Estimated speed drifts. Keep tests short, use similar conditions, and record actual race times and laps separately.

Troubleshooting: If the receiver shows CAR, reset it while holding A. If CSV import fails, confirm you exported MY_DATA from the car board and not the USB receiver. The uploaded HEX is supplied by your team; it has not been hardware-verified here.

MIVD Telemetry Lab — User Manual

Welcome! This lab helps you test your RC car, understand measurements, compare changes, and decide what to improve. You do not need previous telemetry experience.

1. Start with sample data

  1. Click Load sample runs to practice with simulated recordings.
  2. Use Active run to select a test and Compare with to choose another.
  3. Look at each graph and read What it means and What to look for.

2. Record a real vehicle run

  1. Secure the micro:bit flat on the RC car with its top edge pointing forward.
  2. Place the car on level ground and keep it still while pressing A to calibrate and start recording.
  3. Drive the test course. Press B after stopping to end the recording.
  4. Connect the vehicle micro:bit to a computer, open its MY_DATA page, and export the CSV file.
  5. In this dashboard, click Import CSV files and choose the exported file.

Important: Keep the micro:bit securely mounted and away from moving parts. The second micro:bit is the wireless receiver for MakeCode live viewing; this website analyzes the recorded CSV after the run.

3. Understand the main measurements

MeasurementPlain-English meaning
Forward accelerationHow strongly the car speeds up or slows down along its forward direction.
Lateral accelerationSideways acceleration during turns and other side-to-side motion.
Pitch and rollEstimated forward/backward and side-to-side tilt; readings are affected by driving forces.
ImpactA sudden large accelerometer reading that might indicate a bump or collision.
Motion/vibration proxyA measure of motion compared with the starting position, including acceleration and turns.
Estimated speedAn experimental calculation from acceleration, not a verified speedometer reading.

4. Choose the competition goal

In the Engineering Testing section, choose Agility, Mini Oval, Top Speed, Efficiency, Pit Stop, or Sensor Showcase. The dashboard explains which results to examine for that event. Enter official-style performance results such as lap counts or timed runs manually where needed.

5. Save your vehicle setup

Record the driver, tires, spur and pinion teeth, shock oil, differential setup, battery, surface, and test conditions. Give each test a clear name such as “Stock tires — trial 1.” Save a test record after importing its telemetry. The spur/pinion calculator shows the external gear ratio; it does not calculate the full drivetrain ratio.

6. Compare fair tests

  1. Run a baseline test with the original setup.
  2. Change one thing (for example, pinion teeth or tires).
  3. Repeat the same course with similar conditions and, when possible, the same driver.
  4. Use Guided A/B Comparison to compare the results.
  5. Repeat each setup several times before deciding which is better.

7. Make an engineering decision

Complete the Claim–Evidence–Reasoning fields: Claim: which setup should work better? Evidence: what measured values or times support that? Reasoning: why do those results support your choice? Include what you will test next. Use the report download or print control to save your work.

8. Save your work

Use Export project backup in the engineering section to keep a portable copy of your saved testing records. Browser storage may be cleared, so do not rely on it as your only backup. Export analysis CSVs and reports separately as needed.

9. Data reliability and troubleshooting

  • No graphs? Import a CSV from the vehicle micro:bit, or click Load sample runs.
  • Unusual values? Check mounting orientation, stationary calibration, timestamps, and whether the car hit a bump.
  • Speed still rises after stopping? This is normal sensor drift; do not use the estimate to certify top speed.
  • Tests disagree? Check drivers, surfaces, battery conditions, and repeat the trials.
  • No radio data? Make sure both micro:bits use radio group 42 and the receiver is connected to MakeCode's serial viewer.

The goal: Observe → Compare → Decide → Test again. The best vehicle setup is the one that performs reliably in the event you are preparing for, not necessarily the one with the biggest sensor reading.

Quick start · How to read your data

Start here: Read → Compare → Decide

1. Read the graph: Look for high points, low points, and changes over time. Each section explains what you are seeing.

2. Compare two runs: Select two runs. Test one change at a time, such as different tires or a different driving style. Keep the track and test as similar as possible.

Experimental speed estimate

mph · drift-prone
What it meansThis is speed estimated by adding accelerometer readings over time. Errors build up, so the number can be wrong even when the car is stopped.What to look for: Look at the shape of the curve, but do not use its peak to declare which car is fastest.

Lateral acceleration

m/s² · turning forces
What it meansSide-to-side acceleration occurs during turns. Larger values may indicate sharper or faster cornering, but they do not automatically mean better grip.What to look for: Compare the same turn with two tire setups and look for repeatable patterns.

What it means: A higher numerical ratio usually provides more wheel torque and lower wheel speed at the same motor RPM. A lower numerical ratio usually gives more potential wheel speed but less torque multiplication. Actual acceleration, speed, and motor temperature also depend on the motor, battery, tires, and track.
How to use this in a test: Run Setup A, change only the gearing, and repeat the same driving test with Setup B. Compare acceleration, braking, and stability graphs. Do not use the micro:bit's estimated top speed alone to judge which gearing is faster.

Formula: external ratio = spur teeth ÷ pinion teeth. This is not the complete drivetrain final-drive ratio; internal transmission and differential reductions are not included. Check your vehicle's gearing limits and motor temperatures before changing gears.
